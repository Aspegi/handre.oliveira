\section{Introduction}

Safety cases are the primary mechanism by which aviation authorities
(EASA, FAA) assess whether an autonomous system is acceptably safe.
A safety case is a structured argument, typically expressed in Goal
Structuring Notation (GSN)~\cite{kelly1998}, that links high-level
safety goals to concrete evidence. For unmanned aerial vehicles
(UAVs), the safety case must cover the entire envelope: speed,
altitude, obstacle clearance, and failure modes.

The problem is that safety cases are \emph{static}. They are written
once, reviewed once, and then disconnected from the runtime behaviour
they are meant to assure. When the drone flies, nothing in the safety
case changes — even if a sensor degrades, a motor fails, or the
operator sends an unsafe command. The evidence is frozen in time.

This disconnect has two consequences. First, the safety case cannot
detect when the system drifts outside the envelope it was certified
for. Second, there is no mechanism to \emph{regenerate} the safety
case when requirements change. A single-line change in a requirement
(e.g., \texttt{speed\_max: 10.0} to \texttt{speed\_max: 8.0}) requires
manual updates to the tests, the GSN nodes, and the audit trail —
a process that is error-prone and rarely performed.

We present SCC (Safety Case Compiler), a tool that compiles safety
requirements into executable evidence. SCC takes a small DSL of
requirements (speed, altitude, obstacle clearance) and produces
three artefacts per requirement:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A Rust test stub that verifies the invariant at runtime.
    \item A GSN node that links the requirement to its evidence.
    \item A cryptographic block in a hash chain (CertChain) that
          records the compiled requirement.
\end{enumerate}

The key property is \emph{single source of truth}: changing one line
in the requirements file regenerates all three artefacts and
re-validates the chain. There is no manual synchronisation, and no
possibility of the GSN node disagreeing with the test.

We evaluate SCC on a 3-requirement UAV envelope (speed $\leq 10$~m/s,
altitude in $[5, 120]$~m, obstacle clearance $\geq 2$~m). The
compiler produces 3 tests, 3 GSN nodes, and a 3-block chain in
under 100~ms. A single-line change regenerates all artefacts and
the chain re-validates. SCC is implemented in Rust (\texttt{no\_std}
compatible) and tested with 10 unit tests.

The contributions of this paper are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A DSL for safety requirements that compiles to executable
          evidence (tests + GSN + chain blocks).
    \item A compiler architecture that guarantees consistency between
          the three artefacts.
    \item An integration with a hash chain that makes the safety case
          tamper-evident and auditable.
    \item An evaluation on a UAV envelope, including a demonstration
          that a single-line change regenerates all artefacts.
\end{itemize}

The paper is organised as follows. Section~II reviews safety cases
and runtime verification. Section~III presents the SCC design.
Section~IV describes the implementation. Section~V evaluates SCC
on the UAV envelope. Section~VI discusses the relation to DO-326A
and open problems. Section~VII concludes.
